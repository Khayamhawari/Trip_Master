Gateway of India with TripMaster

History

The Gateway of India is an iconic monument located in Mumbai, built in 1924 to commemorate the visit of King George V and Queen Mary to India in 1911. Over the years, it has become a symbol of Mumbai and a popular gathering spot for locals and tourists.

Location

Located on the waterfront in the Apollo Bunder area in South Mumbai, the Gateway of India overlooks the Arabian Sea and stands next to the Taj Mahal Palace Hotel.

How to Reach

You can reach the Gateway of India easily via Mumbai’s local train system (Churchgate or CST stations) or by cab/auto from any part of Mumbai. Ferries to Elephanta Caves also depart from here.

Best Time to Visit

Visit between November and February when the weather is pleasant. Early morning or evening visits provide the best lighting and crowd-free views.

Things to Do

  • Enjoy scenic views and photography at the monument.
  • Take a ferry to Elephanta Caves from the jetty nearby.
  • Explore the historic Taj Mahal Palace Hotel next door.
  • Shop local handicrafts from Colaba Causeway.
  • Relish Mumbai street food at nearby stalls.

Local Cuisine

Mumbai’s food scene around the Gateway is vibrant and diverse. Try:

  • Vada Pav
  • Pav Bhaji
  • Seafood at Leopold Cafe
  • Chaats and Kulfi
  • Irani Chai and Bun Maska

Entry Fees

There is no entry fee to visit the Gateway of India. Ferry rides are ticketed separately based on destination.

Opening Hours

The Gateway of India is open 24/7 for public visits. Ferries operate from 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM.

Travel Tips

  • Arrive early to avoid crowds and heat.
  • Keep cash for ferry rides and street shopping.
  • Don’t forget sunscreen and water during daytime visits.
  • Respect local customs and maintain cleanliness.

Safety Tips

  • Watch out for pickpockets in crowded areas.
  • Be careful with street vendors and verify prices beforehand.
  • Follow ferry safety instructions strictly.
