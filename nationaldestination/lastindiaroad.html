Last Indian Road with TripMaster

History

The Last Indian Road refers to the stretch of road leading to the India-China border in the remote region of Ladakh or Arunachal Pradesh. These roads are maintained by the Border Roads Organisation and offer a breathtaking journey through untouched terrain, often visited by those seeking adventure and patriotism.

Location

Depending on the route taken, the Last Indian Road can be found in Tawang (Arunachal Pradesh) or Ladakh (near Demchok or Hanle), giving a final glimpse of India’s landscape before the international boundary.

How to Reach

You can reach the Last Indian Road by first flying into Leh (Ladakh) or Tezpur (Arunachal), and then proceeding by road with required permits. A 4x4 vehicle is recommended for the rugged terrain.

Best Time to Visit

May to October is the best time to visit these remote roads, as snowfall and harsh weather block routes during winter months.

Things to Do

  • Take a drive to the last accessible point on Indian soil.
  • Visit nearby army outposts and war memorials.
  • Enjoy high-altitude photography and stargazing.
  • Experience cultural villages en route.
  • Feel the thrill of riding through one of the remotest areas in India.

Local Cuisine

The cuisine in these remote areas is simple and hearty. Common dishes include:

  • Thukpa
  • Momos
  • Butter Tea
  • Yak Cheese & Tsampa
  • Maggi at high-altitude stalls

Entry Fees

Entry is free, but Inner Line Permits are required for Indian citizens, and Protected Area Permits for foreign nationals.

Opening Hours

Accessible during daylight hours only. Military regulations may restrict access during specific periods.

Travel Tips

  • Acclimatize properly before visiting high-altitude locations.
  • Carry permits and multiple ID proofs.
  • Keep extra fuel and warm clothing.
  • Travel in a group for safety.
  • Respect military guidelines and signage.

Safety Tips

  • Inform someone about your travel route and timeline.
  • Carry medical supplies for altitude sickness.
  • Avoid nighttime travel due to wildlife and terrain risks.
  • Do not cross into restricted or disputed zones.
